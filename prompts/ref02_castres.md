Draw a reference plate of CASTRES architecture, south-west France, in strict
orthographic isometric projection: 30° elevation above the horizon, 45° azimuth —
the 2:1 game isometric where horizontal edges recede at 26.6° from horizontal, a
slope of one down for two across. Same viewing angle everywhere on the plate.

Content: one short row of four adjoining buildings along a stone quay, plus one
detached stone mansion set back behind them, as a clean study plate on pure white.

DRAWING ECONOMY — this is the most important rule. This plate will be attached to
every tile of a city map at a scale where one whole house is only about 80 pixels
wide and a single roof tile would be 2 pixels. Draw at that economy, not at
poster scale:
- Roofs: the roof planes, the ridge, the hips and the eaves line. Suggest the
  curved terracotta tiles with three or four short courses along the eaves only,
  plus a génoise cornice of two stacked tile courses under the eaves. NEVER
  texture the whole roof plane — leave it white.
- Stone: coursing on the top two courses of the quay parapet and quoins at the
  building corners only. NEVER draw every block of a wall or a quay.
- Half-timbering: four to six timbers per façade — corner posts, one sill beam,
  one or two diagonal braces. Not a dense grid.
- Windows: a plain opening with two hinged shutters. No glazing bars, no reveals.
Reduced to 15% of its size, the whole plate must still read as WHITE with black
outlines. No area may turn gray.

The buildings must be recognisably Castres, NOT Paris:
- two or three old half-timbered houses with a clearly overhanging upper floor
  (encorbellement) built directly on the river, their ground floor rising out of
  the water on stone footings, with no quay in front of them;
- plastered façades with tall hinged wooden shutters, two or three storeys only;
- low-pitched roofs of curved terracotta canal tiles with simple brick chimneys;
- one 17th–18th century stone mansion (hôtel particulier) with a carriage porch,
  a slightly taller ashlar façade and a low-pitched tiled roof;
- a cut-stone quay with a low parapet, and one springing of a stone arch bridge.

Do NOT draw: Haussmann apartment blocks, mansard or zinc roofs, Parisian
wrought-iron balconies, slate roofs, glass towers, or any modern building.
