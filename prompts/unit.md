Image {ref01} shows the DRAWING STYLE: hand-inked cartoon, pure black lines on white
paper, uniform line weight, no shading, no grey, no colour.

Image {lines} is a row of real houses in the old town of Castres (Tarn, France), drawn
as a bare volume in isometric projection. Redraw it in the style of image {ref01}.

THE SILHOUETTE IS FIXED. Every outer edge of image {lines} stays exactly where it is:
same outline, same height at every point along the row, same roof slopes, same angle.
Do not add a volume, do not remove one, do not level the roofline, do not make the
building taller or wider. The row is uneven on purpose — that is what the real street
looks like. All you add is surface detail on the faces image {lines} already has.

KEEP THE FRAME. Your drawing occupies exactly the same place in the frame as image
{lines}, at the same size, with the same white margin all around. Do not zoom in, do
not re-centre, do not crop. The background stays PURE WHITE AND EMPTY: no ground, no
street, no pavement, no neighbouring building, no shadow, no vignette. This drawing
will be cut out along its silhouette and pasted onto a map, so anything outside the
building is thrown away.

THE BOTTOM EDGE IS THE GROUND. The lowest line of the shape in image {lines} is where
the building meets the pavement. Draw NOTHING BELOW IT — no extra storey, no steps, no
plinth, no terrace, and above all no arcade hanging underneath the building. An arcade,
where one is asked for, is cut INTO the ground floor of the volume you are given: its
arches rise from that bottom edge and their tops stay well inside the shape. Everything
you draw outside the shape of image {lines} is cut away and lost.

WHAT TO ADD, and nothing else:
  - windows on every visible façade, aligned in vertical bays, one row per storey;
    each window a plain rectangle taller than wide with a moulded stone surround,
    and NOTHING ELSE — no shutters, no glazing bars, no balconette. The houses of
    Castres photographed for this map have bare windows in a stone frame;
  - a door or a shopfront at street level, one per house;
  - the party wall between two houses, drawn as a line from ground to ridge, so the
    row reads as separate houses and not as one long building;
  - a moulded cornice under the eaves;
  - two or three short courses of roof tile ALONG THE EAVES ONLY — the rest of the
    roof slope stays bare white paper. Never cover a roof with a grid of tiles;
  - one or two plain chimney stacks on the ridge.

CASTRES, NOT PARIS. Pale ashlar stone laid in visible courses, or plain rendered
plaster. Low-pitched roofs of curved terracotta canal tile with a génoise cornice of
two stacked tile courses. No mansard roof, no dormer window, no cast-iron balcony, no
half-timbering — unless the note below asks for one.

NOTHING MODERN: no glass front, no metal, no concrete, no garage door, no shutter box,
no air conditioner, no solar panel. NO PEOPLE, no animals, no cars, no trees, no street
furniture — they are added later, on other layers. NO LETTERS, no words, no numbers
anywhere in the image; a shop sign may carry a small drawn emblem, never text.

TWO TONES ONLY: black ink, white paper. No grey, no wash, no hatching, no stippling,
no tint, not one coloured pixel. Every surface is defined by its outline alone.
